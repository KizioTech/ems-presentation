Multi-Objective
Three objectives, one dispatch decision
The MOLS algorithm simultaneously minimises three conflicting objectives for each incident:

f₁ (Travel + Cost) — combines time-dependent travel time τij(t) with weighted operational cost λ·cij. Lower is faster & cheaper.

f₂ (Reliability Cost) — penalises variance in travel time through γ (variability factor) and the Braess structural vulnerability φ. Routes through high-congestion feeder roads carry disproportionate f₂.

f₃ (Resilience Score) — accumulates edge criticality ρ along the path. A high f₃ means the route depends heavily on structurally critical edges — a network risk.

INC_006 (critical, evening peak) exhibits the most extreme f₂ due to feeder-road vulnerability. INC_014 shows the highest f₁ because its 9-hop path spans the widest physical distance.
f₁(P) = Σ [τᵢⱼ(t) + λ·cᵢⱼ]
f₂(P) = Σ vᵢⱼ(t)
f₃(P) = β·Σ ρᵢⱼ
Section B
Braess Paradox — Highway Upgrade Impact
// RELIABILITY COST f₂: BASELINE vs BRAESS-ADJUSTED (FEEDER ROUTES ONLY)
Braess's Paradox
When a faster road makes the network worse
Braess's Paradox is a counter-intuitive network phenomenon: adding or upgrading a road can increase overall congestion for all users. In this simulation, six trunk-class edges were upgraded to highway standard (E_H), making them time-invariant (congestion multiplier = 1.0 across all periods).

However, the 71 feeder edges (E_F) connecting intersections to these highways experienced a structural vulnerability penalty φ ∈ [0.25, 0.70], modifying the reliability objective:
v*ᵢⱼ(t) = τᵢⱼ(t)·(τᵢⱼ(t)/τ°ᵢⱼ)²·γᵢⱼ·(1 + φᵢⱼ)
INC_006 suffers the largest absolute increase (+7.57 units, +39.2%), occurring during evening peak when feeder roads are already at ×2.5 market congestion. INC_001 shows a +59.8% increase — demonstrating that short critical-priority routes can be severely penalised if they traverse even one high-φ feeder segment.

Time-Dependent
When incidents strike — and how the network responds
The Zomba network uses five time periods with piecewise-linear congestion functions. Market roads — concentrated in the CBD — experience up to 2.5× baseline travel time during evening peak (15:00–19:00).

Two of the three critical incidents occurred near midnight (INC_001 at 23:12, INC_004 at 21:10) when congestion drops to near-free flow — this partially explains their relatively low f₂ values despite high priority.

INC_006 (critical, 17:59) is the notable exception: it struck at the peak of market congestion (×2.5), producing the highest f₂ in the entire dataset. INC_008 (urgent, 16:07) similarly suffers elevated reliability cost due to feeder dependence during evening peak.

Fleet Analysis
AMB_01 carries the fleet — AMB_04 sits idle
The sequential priority-based dispatch policy produces a heavily uneven workload distribution across the 5-vehicle fleet:

AMB_01 (priority rank 1, base: Dispatch_A) handles 5 dispatches including the complex 9-hop INC_014 route. Its sequential availability explains the long queue delay — INC_007, INC_011, INC_013, and INC_014 all departed well after their call time.

AMB_04 (priority rank 4) receives zero dispatches — a direct consequence of the strict priority ordering. In real-world scenarios, proximity-weighted dispatch policies would redistribute load more equitably.

The dispatch delay is most visible for INC_008 (urgent): called at 16:07 but departed at 18:37 — a 2.5-hour queuing delay due to AMB_03's prior commitment to INC_006.

Pareto Front Size — Solution Space Richness
// BUBBLE: f₁ vs f₂ · BUBBLE SIZE = PARETO FRONT SIZE · COLOUR = SEVERITY
Pareto Optimality
Larger bubbles = richer routing choices
The Multi-Objective Label-Setting (MOLS) algorithm retains all Pareto-non-dominated label pairs throughout the graph traversal. The resulting Pareto front size reflects the topological richness around each origin-destination pair.

A pareto_size = 1 means only a single route survived objective pruning — the MOLS found no meaningful trade-off alternatives. This occurs for geographically simple O-D pairs (INC_001: 37→26, direct edge) or zero-distance incidents (co-located vehicle and scene).

pareto_size = 6 (INC_006, INC_014) indicates high routing flexibility — the dispatcher has genuine choices between faster-but-costlier and slower-but-more-reliable routes. This is where policy selection (TIME vs RELIABILITY vs PARETO) has the most impact.

Objective Space
The fundamental trade-off revealed
This scatter reveals the Pareto frontier in objective space: no single route can minimise all three objectives simultaneously. Key observations:

Feeder routes cluster high in f₂ — the Braess vulnerability pushes red points upward regardless of f₁. A dispatcher optimising for reliability (f₂) should avoid feeder-dependent paths even when they appear time-optimal.

Large points (high f₃ resilience) tend to fall in the middle of the scatter — routes with high criticality exposure are neither the fastest nor the slowest, but they introduce structural risk: if a critical edge is removed, these routes have no alternative.

The ideal Pareto solution would occupy the bottom-left of this chart with a small point — INC_002 and INC_015 come closest, representing short urgent incidents resolved via low-criticality standard routes at night-to-midday congestion levels.
